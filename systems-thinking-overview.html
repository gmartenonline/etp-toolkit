Finding Leverage Points: Reversing Vicious Cycles

A five-part framework for using systems thinking to help communities facing ecological decline that is pushing them toward environmental and social collapse. Where a turnabout is possible, it starts with strategic choices: positive tipping point levers that intentionally create virtuous cycles powerful enough to tip the balance towards harmony.

Everywhere communities depend on their lands and waters for survival, people are fighting the damage to them. Their efforts are well intentioned, but many do little more than swim against the current. They soften the blows while the ecosystem keeps spiraling towards collapse. Changing the direction of that spiral takes more than managing symptoms of decline one at a time. Vicious cycles of decline take hold when problems for the ecosystem are built into how communities work, live, and subsist. These are the cycles that need to be broken.

Many communities have shown that even the most dire conditions can be turned around. Applied in the right places, force can reverse the current and send the spiral toward regeneration instead of collapse. Those well chosen sites of intervention are EcoTipping Points, or points in the human-environment system where a turnabout can be generated if the right changes are set in motion. Those successful communities wielded the force of a positive tipping point lever. Their actions successfully set in motion the changes needed to make a switch from decline to restoration and sustainability. Not all restorative actions carry enough force to tip conditions in the human-environment system from decline to restoration, but our case studies have provided crucial insights into the qualities of positive tipping point levers and what it takes to put them into action.

Our Systems Thinking framework gives you the tools to devise positive tipping point levers. With it, communities can use a systems lens to understand what's driving their ecological decline, then find well-chosen actions that put the power of EcoTipping Points to work and create a turnabout towards restoration.

The Five-Part Process

Using the lens of Systems Thinking, the five steps give you the tools to:

  • UNDERSTAND in general why EcoTipping Points can help with problem-solving
  • RECOGNIZE the web of interconnected problems across the ecosystem and human social system at a particular location
  • MAP relationships of cause and effect with diagrams that reveal where vicious cycles are coming into play
  • REVERSE it by using those diagrams to identify root causes driving decline, sites for effective intervention (EcoTipping Points), and feasible actions with enough force to trigger systemic change (Positive Tipping Point Levers)
  • LOCK IT IN with strategies to make gains sustainable

Our strategies for identifying effective modes of intervention draw from patterns reflected in more than 100 case studies that we have documented from around the world, case studies where local communities have found effective levers for positive change.

Lock It In
